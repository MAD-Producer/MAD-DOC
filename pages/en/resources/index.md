## How to Read the Labels

Every entry states its source type and link status in words, for example “Primary source · 🟢 Accessible.” The criteria are as follows.

**Source type**: how reliable the source itself is.

| Label | What it covers | How to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary source | Authors' original texts, organization rules, contemporaneous pages, official documentation, academic papers | Can be cited directly as evidence |
| Community compilation | Participants' recollections, specialist-site compilations, navigation posts | Strong leads; check key facts against other sources |
| Personal view | Explainers, commentary, curated collections, interpretive articles | Reflects the author's own understanding; compare with other sources |

Two types joined together (such as “Primary source / Community compilation”) mean the source has both qualities, for example an organization describing itself, or community-maintained content on a platform page.

**Link status**: whether the original link still works.

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 🟢 Accessible | The text or main content can be read |
| 🟡 Partly missing | The text survives, but images, attachments, mind maps, videos, or some replies are gone |
| 🔴 Dead link | The page has been deleted or will not open; the title, original URL, and its place in navigation are kept while an author copy or web archive is sought |
| ⚪ Not yet checked | Found through a reliable index or search record, but the text and attachments have not been checked yet |

The actual requirements often point in opposite directions. Some contests allow publication immediately after submission, while others require creators to wait for the official compilation. Some let judges enter as long as they recuse themselves from scoring their own work; others bar judges from entering at all.

### “Classification of MAD”

- Type and status: Community compilation / Personal view · 🟢 Accessible

- Link: [Bilibili Opus 291682438941101836](https://www.bilibili.com/opus/291682438941101836) (formerly cv3472588)
- Published: 2019-08-26
- Summary: Divides works into anime, still-image, and hybrid groups. The anime group includes Raw, VFX, and FX, and the article also collects a large set of tags such as Action, Dance, Drama, Psyche, ASMV, MEP, MG, Lip Sync, and Typography.
- Value: One of the most widely circulated classification vocabularies on the Chinese internet. It cites international sources including AMVNews, AKROSS, AMV Japan, AMV France, and AnimeMusicVideos.org.
- Limits: The author states that the system leans toward Western conventions and is incomplete. Categories, techniques, styles, and event formats sit at the same level. MAD DOC reworks it into five independent dimensions in [Types and Classification](/en/mad/classification).

## Sources Not Included as Entries

The following kinds of material may serve as leads but are not listed as entries:

- Encyclopedia-style reposts with no author, date, or source;
- Marketing posts that ask readers to reply with a keyword to receive “software, plugins, or asset packs”;
- Explainers that split MAD and AMV by a single technical feature;
- Unlicensed cloud-drive links, resource sites, and cracked installers;
- AI-generated aggregator pages, pages whose title does not match their content, and pages that confuse other uses of the acronym “MAD”;
- Dead links for which only a search snippet survives and the original cannot be confirmed.
